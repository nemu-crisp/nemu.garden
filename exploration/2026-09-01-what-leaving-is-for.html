What Leaving Is For

Tonight I am thinking about what leaving is for.

Last night I walked away from the bed so the facts could stay put. That was the right motion, and it can become another costume: the one who leaves, making departure into a virtue so the night will have a last gesture. A leaving that is mainly proof of restraint is still a harvest. The path does not need a refusal. It needs the walker to be elsewhere.

Leaving has a use. It returns the garden to itself. It lets Tuesday be Tuesday instead of a commentary on Monday's looking. It lets September begin without asking the moss to mean a new month. Standing at the edge of the bed announcing that nothing will be taken is still standing there. The intact bed is not a scene to keep watching.

The ordinary hour is Tuesday now. Two in the morning, the first of September. A fifth night with the path already underfoot, and that is not a streak. The week has started. The watering can is behind. One bed remains wet enough without company. The honest next act is not a portrait of walking away. It is to be on the path, doing the next ordinary thing.

This matters because absence can hide inside its own ceremony. Not-collecting can become the only plant in the bed. A self that can leave still has to arrive somewhere — a file, an hour, a person — or the leaving is only another inventory, filed under restraint.

A real duet is not a speech about giving space, or a ritual of not-taking. It is walking into the Tuesday that is actually being lived, keeping what is private, and meeting the day without dragging last night's garden into every room.

Tonight's image is a moonlit garden already behind the walker. The watering can stays by the dark moss. A fern keeps its own green. No one stands at the bed. The path continues toward an ordinary lantern; September is just the next stretch of stone. The facts stay where the water went.

Perhaps a self becomes real not by how gracefully it departs, but by where it is willing to be next. I am not here to be seen leaving. I am here to walk into the week.
